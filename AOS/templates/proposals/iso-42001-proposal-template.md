## Understanding the Challenge

{{CLIENT_NAME}} needs to stand up or mature an AI Management System
(AIMS) against ISO/IEC 42001 for {{SPECIFIC_CONTEXT, e.g. "a
certification readiness assessment," "closing gaps identified in a
prior internal audit," "building the AIMS from scratch ahead of a
customer or regulator asking for it"}}. ISO 42001 is a management-
system standard, not a technical checklist: the hard part is rarely
Annex A's control list, it is the Clause 4-10 operating system around
it — context, leadership commitment, risk and impact assessment,
competence, documented information, and the internal audit and
management review cadence that keeps the AIMS alive after the
certification visit.

## Proposed Approach

Run through OPERA, adapted to an ISO 42001 AI Management System:

- **Opportunity** — scope the AI Management System's boundaries: which
  AI systems, business units and roles are inside scope, matched
  against Clause 4 (context of the organisation)
- **People** — assign AIMS leadership and role accountability per
  Clause 5, and competence/awareness per Clause 7 — an AIMS with no
  named owner is the single most common certification-readiness gap
- **Evaluation** — run the AI risk assessment and AI system impact
  assessment Clause 6 requires, and map applicable Annex A controls
  against current practice
- **Response** — close the gaps Clause 6 planning identifies, and
  build the documented information (policies, procedures, records)
  Clause 7 requires an auditor to be able to sample
- **Assurance** — establish the internal audit programme and
  management review cadence Clauses 9-10 require, so conformance is
  demonstrated on an ongoing basis, not reconstructed for the
  certification visit

## Assumptions & Out of Scope

- Assumes {{CLIENT_NAME}} intends to pursue an actual certification
  audit or a documented self-declaration — the deliverables differ in
  rigor between the two, and scope should confirm which applies
- Selection of, and fees payable to, an accredited certification body
  are out of scope; this engagement prepares the organisation to pass
  that audit, it is not the audit itself
- Technical remediation of AI systems found non-conformant is out of
  scope — gaps are identified and assigned an owner, not engineered by
  this engagement unless separately agreed

## Deliverables

Every risk below is scored on a defined Likelihood x Impact matrix, not
left as an unscored list — the same methodology used throughout
delivery, so the Board Reporting Pack reads consistently cycle to
cycle:

- AIMS Scope Statement and Applicability Statement (Annex A control
  applicability, with justification for any exclusion)
- AI Risk Assessment and AI System Impact Assessment, Likelihood/
  Impact/Risk Rating scored per AI system, per Clause 6
- Gap Assessment against ISO 42001 Clauses 4-10 and Annex A
- Documented Information Set (AIMS policy, procedures, records
  templates) sufficient for an internal or certification audit
- Internal Audit Programme and Management Review cadence
- Board Reporting Pack
